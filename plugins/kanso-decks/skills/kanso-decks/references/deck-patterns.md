# Deck Patterns Research — structure for Kanso's deck-generation skill

Source: deck.gallery catalog extract (50 decks, slide-level captions only; no visuals). This document extracts structure, not content.

## Method and confidence

- **Core agency set (n = 14)**, hand-labelled slide by slide: Something Else, Studio Linear, Reece Evans, Studio Freight Capes 2024, BB Agency, Andreas Maris, Zapion, Refresh, McKinley, Mindset, Smith & Diction, Studio Treble, Emma, Satrio Arkan. Median length **22.5 slides** (range 9–56).
- Studio Freight appears three times in the catalog (Capes 2024, Capabilities, Capabilities 2023) with near-identical 55–56 slide structures. Only Capes 2024 is counted, to avoid triple-weighting.
- **Caption quality varies.** Studio Freight Capabilities 2023, Andreas Maris, Mindset, Studio Treble, and parts of McKinley and Refresh have generic, template-like captions ("case study results", "process and methodology"). Treat their internal sequencing as lower confidence. Notably, the 2023 Studio Freight captions describe challenge/results slides that the more specific Capes 2024 captions of the same deck do not show.
- **Secondary set** (narrative patterns only): Reflektor WebAR guide, SPACE10 catalogues, Anyways report, Linear, Fernand, Superlist, Perplexity Ads, Diagram.
- Position = (slide n − 1) / (slide count − 1). 0 = first slide, 1 = last.

## 2. Deck archetypes for Kanso

### 2.1 Intro / capabilities deck (observed, strong evidence)

**Observed length:** 9–56 slides. The short studio cluster runs 9–23 (Andreas Maris, Smith & Diction, Mindset, Treble, Something Else, Emma, Zapion, Studio Linear). The long portfolio cluster runs 40–56 (BB, SF, Reece Evans, Satrio). For a first-contact deck, **14–24 slides** is the sweet spot.

**Most common ordering:**
1. Cover — *universal (14/14)*
2. Agenda — optional, only in decks over 30 slides
3. Positioning statement, 1–3 slides — *near-universal (10/14)*
4. About / origin plus credentials or metrics — common (5/14 each)
5. Values / differentiators (often a 3-pillar slide) — optional
6. Services overview — *near-universal (9/14)*
7. Service deep dives, one per discipline — optional (4/14); fits Kanso's 4 services
8. Process / phases — *near-universal (9/14)*
9. Section opener: Work — common in longer decks
10. Client logo wall — optional (5/14)
11. Case studies: 3–6 cases, each 1–8 slides — *universal in practice (13/14)*
12. Testimonial(s) — common (6/14)
13. Team / culture — optional (3/14)
14. Engagement model or packages — optional (4/14 show prices)
15. Contact — *near-universal (10/14)*
16. Closing card — common (9/14)

**Two sub-patterns:**
- *Story-first* (SF, BB, Zapion, Refresh): positioning, then services and process, then cases, then team and contact.
- *Work-first* (Something Else, Emma, Reece Evans, Satrio): a brief intro, then projects dominate 60–90% of the deck, and process or contact are squeezed at the end. Emma interleaves process and pricing between projects.

**Best examples:** BB Agency Company Deck (https://www.deck.gallery/bb-agency-company-deck/), Refresh Studios Capabilities Deck 2024 (https://www.deck.gallery/refresh-studios-capabilities-deck-2024/), Zapion Design Studios Agency Deck (https://www.deck.gallery/zapion-design-studios-agency-deck/), Something Else Capabilities Deck 2021 (https://www.deck.gallery/something-else-capabilities-2021/) as the compact reference.

### 2.2 Proposal (mostly inferred, based on 1 true proposal)

The only deck in the catalog labelled as a proposal is **McKinley** (46 slides). Evidence for pricing and packaging comes from Studio Linear, Emma, BB, and Mindset. The problem-to-solution-to-ask spine comes from sales and investor decks (Linear, Fernand, Superlist, Perplexity).

**Observed McKinley spine (condensed):** cover → executive summary/vision → credential with the prospect's own sector → the client's problem → traditional vs integrated comparison → proposed model → services → work → process → metrics → per-service detail → case → cost and time savings → timeline → differentiators → rollout approach → engagement model → investment and ROI → next steps → contact. Testimonials recur about every 6–8 slides (5 in total).

**Recommended Kanso proposal sequence** (O = observed in ≥1 deck, I = inferred):
1. Cover with client name and project title — O
2. Summary: what we propose, in one sentence — O (McKinley)
3. Our understanding: the client's situation and problem — O (McKinley, Linear, Fernand)
4. Goals / success criteria — I
5. Proposed approach or solution, optionally as a before/after comparison — O (McKinley)
6. Scope by service (Brand / Design / Experience / Development) — O (Studio Linear per-service pricing slides)
7. Process and phases with durations — O (Emma: 4 phases over 5 weeks)
8. Timeline / milestones — O (McKinley)
9. Relevant proof: 1–2 short cases plus a testimonial — O (McKinley, Studio Linear)
10. Team assigned — I (team appears in 3 capabilities decks)
11. Investment: packages or tiers — O (Studio Linear 4 tiers, Emma 3 packages, BB rate card)
12. Add-ons / optional scope — O (Studio Linear, Emma)
13. Engagement model and terms, e.g. retainer vs project — O (Studio Linear social retainer, McKinley)
14. Next steps — O (McKinley, Treble)
15. Contact / closing — O

**Near-universal (by inference):** 1, 3, 5, 6, 11, 14. **Optional:** 4, 9, 10, 12.
**Length:** McKinley's 46 is long for a studio proposal. Inferred target: **12–20 slides**.
Kanso decision (v1.3, 2026-09-16): a short 9-slide proposal — see `deck-flows.md`.
**Examples:** McKinley Studios Pitch Deck (https://www.deck.gallery/mckinley-pitch-deck-retail-rollout/), Studio Linear Packages & Pricing (https://www.deck.gallery/studio-linear-packages-pricing/), Emma Capabilities Deck 2023 (https://www.deck.gallery/emma-philips-capabilities-deck/).

### 2.3 Case study deck (observed inside capabilities decks, not as standalone decks)

No standalone case-study deck exists in the catalog. The structure below comes from case blocks inside SF, BB, Reece Evans, Satrio, and Zapion.

**Recommended sequence:**
1. Cover: client, project, year, services — O
2. Context / brief / challenge — O (Reece Evans brief slide, BB case titles phrased as the challenge; SF 2023 captions, low confidence)
3. Approach / strategy, e.g. research or journey mapping — O (BB CVMaker, Mindset)
4. Core idea or mark rationale (the symbol and what it means) — O (Zapion ×2)
5. Identity system: logo, type, colour, grid — O (SF, Satrio)
6. Applications: web, product UI, OOH, merch, packaging — O (SF, BB)
7. Product / experience screens across devices — O (BB: phone, tablet, wearable)
8. Results and outcomes — O (BB, Zapion deliverables)
9. Testimonial — O (SF, every case)
10. Credits / links / next case — O (SF "notations")

**Length:** 8–14 slides standalone (inferred by extending the 5–8 slide blocks). Universal: 1, 5/6 (visuals), 8 or 9. Optional: 3, 4, 7, 10.
**Examples:** Studio Freight Capes 2024 (https://www.deck.gallery/studio-freight-capes-2024/), BB Agency Company Deck (https://www.deck.gallery/bb-agency-company-deck/).

### 2.4 Project kickoff (inferred, no direct example)

No kickoff deck exists in the catalog. The evidence is fragmentary: BB's full-cycle website blueprint slide (which names a kickoff and risk-assessment stage) and its "what to expect" slide, the process decks (Refresh's phase-per-slide section, Smith & Diction's 10-step framework with a slide on the presentation/feedback step), the SPACE10 catalogues' numbered how-it-works and usage-guideline slides, and McKinley's timeline and quality-control slides.

**Recommended sequence (all I unless marked):**
1. Cover: project name, client, date
2. Why we're here: project goals in one sentence — O (positioning-style statement)
3. Scope recap (what's in, what's out) — O (Studio Linear, Emma add-ons define scope edges)
4. Team and roles on both sides — O (team slides)
5. Process overview — O (9/14 decks)
6. One slide per phase: activities, deliverables, client inputs — O (Refresh, BB)
7. Timeline and milestones — O (McKinley, Emma duration)
8. What to expect / ways of working: communication, feedback rounds, tools — O (BB expectations, Smith & Diction feedback step, Zapion tools)
9. Feedback and approval protocol — O (Smith & Diction)
10. What we need from you: access, assets, stakeholders — I (mirrors SPACE10 guideline slides)
11. Risks / open questions — O (BB mentions risk assessment)
12. Next steps: first two weeks — O (McKinley, Treble)
13. Contacts

**Length:** 10–16 slides (inferred). Near-universal: 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 12.

## 3. Case-study internal structure

**Slides per case (observed):**
- Studio Freight: **8 per case, fixed** across 6 cases (opener → 6 visuals → testimonial/notes)
- BB Agency: **4–5** (opener → 2–3 visuals → results or closing line)
- Reece Evans: **2–6**, median 3 (opener → visuals)
- Satrio Arkan: **1–7**, varying with project weight
- Zapion: **2–3** (opener → mark rationale → deliverables)
- Something Else, Refresh, Studio Linear, Emma: **1 slide per project** (catalogue style)

**Typical internal sequence:** opener (client and one-line framing) → brief or challenge, often folded into the opener's subtitle → visuals ordered from identity to applications to digital → results → testimonial. In the specific captions, dedicated challenge slides are rare. The challenge is usually carried in the case title itself, e.g. BB's case titles describe the problem being solved.

**How results are shown:**
- *Business outcomes stated in a closing sentence*: funding raised after the rebrand, platform partner counts (BB, Zapion).
- *Aggregated outcomes before the cases*: BB lists client funding milestones on a single slide before the work section.
- *Testimonial as the result*: SF closes every case with a quote rather than numbers.
- *Deliverables list as the result*: Zapion, Andreas Maris.
- Portfolio-style decks (Reece Evans, Satrio) show **no results at all**, only visuals.

Numeric results are the exception (about 3 of 14 decks).

## 4. Narrative techniques worth reusing

1. **One idea split across consecutive slides.** A positioning sentence is broken over 2–3 slides, and paired principles span two slides. Observed in SF (3 slides) and BB (two principle pairs), and in the sales decks Perplexity and Fernand.
2. **Section openers as chapter breaks.** Useful in decks over 25 slides. Observed in SF, BB, Refresh (6 openers), and Reece Evans (before the logo and personal-work sections).
3. **Agenda that mirrors the openers.** The contents slide and the section dividers use the same labels. Observed in BB, SF, Refresh.
4. **Metrics before cases.** Credentials and outcome numbers prime the reader before the work. Observed in BB (studio metrics, then client outcomes, then case section) and McKinley.
5. **Fixed case template.** Every case gets identical slide count and rhythm, which reads as system and confidence. Observed in SF.
6. **Testimonial closes each case**, or recurs as a rhythm every 6–8 slides. Observed in SF and McKinley.
7. **Educate before selling.** Define the deliverable before showing process and price. Observed in Emma and Smith & Diction (which even opens with it).
8. **Services → process → price → add-ons as a contiguous block.** Observed in Studio Linear and Emma.
9. **Pricing placed after proof.** BB puts rates as the penultimate chapter, after cases and culture. Studio Linear leads with pricing because the deck is explicitly a price list. Choose placement by deck purpose.
10. **Concept rationale slide.** Explain what the mark symbolises before showing applications. Observed in Zapion.
11. **Problem → contrast → solution spine**, borrowed from sales decks. Observed in Linear, Fernand, McKinley (traditional vs integrated).
12. **Closing with a process-themed line.** Smith & Diction's last slide loops back to its process theme.
13. **Values or social-impact slide near the end** as a humanising beat. Observed in Studio Linear (pro-bono initiative) and Emma (brand promise).
14. **Numbered how-it-works steps** for anything the client must do. Observed in SPACE10 (4 steps). Transferable to kickoff.

## 5. Anti-patterns to avoid

- **Excessive length for first contact.** SF (55–56), BB (49), McKinley (46), and Diagram (79) are reference catalogues, not meeting decks. Split them into a short intro deck plus an appendix or case-study decks.
- **Too many section openers.** Refresh spends 6 of 32 slides (~19%) on dividers or intros before content, and its FAQ is introduced by its own divider. Use at most one opener per chapter, and only in decks over 20 slides.
- **Redundant positioning and approach slides.** Refresh has 4 approach-style slides with overlapping claims. Zapion restates positioning on slides 2 and 4. Keep one statement slide.
- **Double closings.** Emma uses 3 closing slides and Zapion 2 contact slides. Merge contact and thank-you into one.
- **Testimonial overuse.** McKinley's 5 standalone testimonials in one deck start to feel like padding.
- **Visual-only cases with no framing.** In Reece Evans and Satrio, most cases have no brief and no outcome. That works for an individual portfolio, but it is weak for a studio pitching strategy and development.
- **Duplicated deck versions.** Three near-identical SF decks suggest version sprawl. The skill should generate from one source of truth.
- **Metrics buried or absent.** Only about 3 of 14 decks quantify results. This is a differentiation opportunity for Kanso.
- **Generic slide titles** that describe the slide type rather than make a claim. The low-information captions are a symptom. Titles should state the takeaway.
